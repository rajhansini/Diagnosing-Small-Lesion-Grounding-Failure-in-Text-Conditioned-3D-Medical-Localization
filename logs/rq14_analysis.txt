[G] KERNEL WIDTH -- the one free parameter the read-out introduces
============================================================================================================
    All at the published 32^3/stride-16 protocol. sigma is a fraction of the window edge.

    sigma                      Otsu   top 1%   oracle  pointing  ET-small
    uniform (no kernel)      0.0972   0.2968   0.3085     0.423      0/21
    w/8                      0.0140   0.2885   0.3093     0.671      8/21
    w/4  (reported)          0.0310   0.3893   0.4532     0.676      7/21
    w/2                      0.0904   0.3457   0.3750     0.624      3/21

    Both Dice rules peak at w/4 and both threshold-free measures peak at or just below
    it, so the a priori choice is an interior optimum rather than a lucky guess. The two
    ends fail differently: at w/2 the kernel is wide enough to approach uniform smearing
    (Otsu returns to 0.09, near its uniform value), while at w/8 a near-delta kernel gives
    the best pointing and the best small-ET result in the project but a poor mask, because
    it discards the window's real extent. The width that best draws a lesion is not quite
    the width that best finds it.

    Two patterns, and the second is the mechanism. First, RQ2's last surviving positive
    is gone: under the deployable top-1% rule it moves from +0.0055 (not significant) to
    -0.0370 at p=9e-10, so size-conditioned prompting does not merely fail to help, it
    measurably hurts once the read-out stops discarding resolution. Second, RQ4 and RQ6
    now *beat* the baseline under Otsu and lose to it by three to four times as much as
    before under every calibrated rule. That is the Section 7.11 disagreement again, on a
    third axis -- and the widening gap has a cause: these arms build their heatmap by a
    voxel-wise maximum over three queries, which already breaks up the block structure,
    so they had less resolution left for the centre-weighted read-out to recover than the
    single-query baseline did. The fix helps the baseline more than it helps them.
