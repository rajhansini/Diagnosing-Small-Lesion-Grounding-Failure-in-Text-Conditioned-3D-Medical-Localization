    The selection favours the ablations, so RQ4's and RQ6's negative verdicts are
    conservative and RQ2's positive one is not.

    WT     large      20    16/20    17/20      20/20        8.12%          12.3x  (p=1.6e-22)

    The headline bin, stated three ways. Small enhancing tumor, n=21:
      peak voxel (argmax corner) inside the lesion ....... 0/21
      peak voxel (plateau centroid) inside the lesion .... 0/21
      peak *block* touches the lesion ................... 9/21  (36x the 1.19% block chance)
    Both point rules agree the model never points at a small enhancing tumor. The block
    rule shows it is not lost either: the winning 16^3 block contains part of the lesion
    in 9 of 21 cases. The failure is resolution *inside* the block, not coarse search --
    which is the architectural reading, measured rather than asserted.

    Holding the window at 8^3 and changing only the stride, 4 -> 8:
      Otsu's mask shrinks from 9.12% to 7.20% of the volume (-21%)
      -- i.e. dropping overlap moves Otsu's cut point toward the truth for a reason that
      has nothing to do with the heatmap being better. That is the whole disagreement:
      Otsu rewards the blockier heatmap because a coarser histogram splits more cleanly,
      while every fixed-fraction rule is scored on spatial resolution it has just lost.
